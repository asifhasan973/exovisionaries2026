# Geology mission economy

The Geology mission has one 10,000-MC spending limit. `src/game/story/moon/geologyBudget.ts` calculates the total from the selected cabin, life support, safety equipment, launch service, transfer-fuel package, landing and return hardware, rover, power, communications, thermal protection, science tools and upgrades. Included platform parts are charged once. The earlier Ice mission keeps its 1,000-credit equipment kit and separate 240-credit crew roster allocation.

The Standard cabin (750 MC), Standard life support (500 MC) and all eight mandatory safety items (890 MC) total 2,140 MC, 3,667 kg, 990 W and 120 authored safety points. A Basic cabin seats only two and blocks departure with a three-person crew. Life-support capacity must cover planned crew-days, including two spare days. The Extended life-support tier also models a larger 500 kWh platform energy store; Standard uses 300 kWh. The crew check offers Compact, Standard and Extended transfer packs; their costs are 1,100, 1,200 and 1,400 MC and their abstract game fuel capacities are 650, 850 and 1,100 units. The fixed stage allocations total 530 units including an emergency reserve. The chosen pack also changes the simulation's service-fuel and mass inputs, so route and Earth-return feasibility are recalculated.

The 100 kg Geology science-payload limit, 350 kg workshop payload cap and 2,000 W integrated power supply check are separate from the MC ledger. The six-part Deep Research science example is 99 kg and 930 MC; with Standard hardware the mission total is 9,300 MC and the planned-craft mass is 7,606 kg. Workshop upgrades, a different crew system, extended supplies or another propulsion pack change those numbers. Science and safety points cannot bypass mandatory survival or return checks.

The five-instrument Balanced reference kit is 38 kg / 390 MC. It omits a sample collector, so players must add a 3 kg / 20 MC scoop or another sample tool before collecting rocks. The budget automatically includes that addition.

MC prices, planning fuel units, spacecraft masses, power ratings and safety points are fictional game-balancing values. The 7,606 kg build ledger is an illustrative selection chart; the existing flight simulator uses its own authored wet-mass and rocket-equation model. The game does not claim NASA flight certification or vendor pricing. Changing a hardware selection invalidates saved checks and departure approval, and restored saves are revalidated against the current rules.
