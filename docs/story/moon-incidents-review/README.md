# Module 8 — adaptive incidents and surface recovery, laptop only

Module 8 extends the running science expedition rather than adding four mandatory quiz pages. A saved name/objective/site scenario seed chooses an environmental incident family. Actual drill/radio/time conditions must still occur. An energy-reserve shortfall can trigger a power decision independently. Each run is capped at two relevant incidents; not every run sees every event. Resets at the same mission configuration do not reroll the scenario.

## Decisions have consequences

- **Dust:** excavation disturbs regolith; there is no Earth-like dust windstorm. An authored dust fraction of 0.6 reduces installed solar output to 70%, increases rover drive load by 12% and limits NIRVSS optical quality to low. Navigation remains on the previously surveyed corridor. Existing evidence is immutable: later repair does not retrospectively improve a measurement. Different depths/sites or a changed optical condition after calibration/cleaning can obtain a new immutable reading. Repeating under the same conditions is blocked, and any later reading at that site earns zero extra instrument points.
- **Packed repair:** optional Surface care kit is physically in the workshop/manifest at 20 game credits and 3 kg, with zero operating power and one cleaning use. The default quick-build kit carries it (985 credits); legacy missions never receive it in flight. At Base, two sheltered surface crew perform a bounded 8-minute cleaning EVA. It spends time, life support and standby energy, adds to the shared 60-minute suit budget and invalidates park/crew confirmations. Some sticky dust remains (0.15 fraction, 92.5% panel output). These are authored balancing values, not NASA equipment prices/masses or cleaning guarantees.
- **Expert repair:** a selected systems engineer and installed NIRVSS allow a 3-minute sensor check where the local link exists. Optical quality can improve to medium but panels stay dusty. No spare electronics or magical new heater/battery is granted.
- **Radio:** the live shared terrain/range predicate opens the alert. More transmit power cannot see through a ridge and cannot deliver an uplink through a broken link. The offered safe action follows the previously loaded route. Fallback return from another incident reverses the exact current leg fraction and traverses a physical path to Base; it is not a teleport. The decision interface represents choosing preloaded rover-controller fallbacks while the presentation is paused, not successfully sending a new message in a blackout. A reconnection is marked resolved only when geometry reconnects the link.
- **Power:** a full-trip or remaining-trip forecast protects the battery reserve and crew home allowance. Before departure a player can select a shorter sample or closer site or charge in actual sunlight; a fitted optional MSolo can be switched off before its analysis. Navigation, heaters and the mandatory science sensors are not freely removed. If a choice is insufficient the underlying forecast still gates starting the trip. A safe early stop returns the rover and preserves collected data rather than inventing a success or spending crew propulsion reserves.
- **Radiation:** a stable space-weather scenario begins after ten virtual minutes of new expedition time, lasting an authored thirty virtual minutes. EVA is blocked throughout. Crew remain sheltered. The robot may carry on under its stored plan, or return to Base and automatically wait out the remaining alert. Waiting spends real virtual time, cabin energy, crew supplies and rover standby energy. Shelter represents reduced exposure qualitatively, not a certified shield, dose forecast or guaranteed radiation-hard rover. No outdoor repair is permitted during the alert.

Menus/hidden tabs/Hint and pending decisions pause the simulation. Repairs preserve the interrupted task and frame. Operating loads use the existing W/Wh integral. Renderer interpolation and narration never advance resources. Sounds are UI/operator feedback, not sound traveling through lunar vacuum.

## Completion, early stop and preparation retry

Science can finish with valid transmitted evidence or an explicit safe early stop. “Safe early stop” is retained for the later Earth-return debrief; it never claims an ice discovery. An isolated preview is clearly assisted and uses the same deployment, science and incident reducers.

Science committed → recovery: park the rover with heaters on, relay **all** onboard records (including observations from earlier optional trips), and confirm the two surface crew sheltered plus one orbiting specialist. These actions spend modeled time and can only succeed at Base with the real crew state. A radiation warning must clear first. Optional cleaning cannot silently rewrite scientific evidence and forces park/crew rechecks. Surface secured ends this module. Lunar ascent/Earth return remain Module 9.

Back opens the paused checkpoint. “Retry before launch” explicitly creates a new Earth preparation attempt from the same blueprint/crew/plan, resets flight/check/incident/evidence receipts and requires fresh approval. It does not buy parts on the Moon.
