# Moon Atlas and Orbit & Signal Lab

Orbit & Signal Lab remains independent. NASA location layers are available as a secondary section of the final Moon mission report. Moon Atlas is removed from primary navigation; its legacy URL remains a clearly labeled reference preview. These views do not modify the story session. Direct development entry: `/?explore=atlas` or `/?explore=signal`. Pages are lazy-loaded from `apps/web/src/features/exploration/Exploration.tsx`. Exploration has its own local field-note key; mission science, fuel, saves and comparisons are untouched.

## Moon Atlas reference layers

The report reuses these grids and source metadata through a lazy, cached reference viewer. Current mission region IDs have no supported Atlas-coordinate mapping; it therefore offers a reference-region preview, never an actual landing/sample point. Projection and sampling helpers live in `packages/engine/src/atlas/`; legacy Atlas sampling reuses them. Grid/image preparation registers with the central loading system. Failures retain report navigation and provide Retry/source links. See [Moon mission report](moon-mission-report.md) for data ownership, saved-report reopening and verification.

The south-polar overview is a 420×420 spherical stereographic grid spanning ±310 km. Longitude zero is up; 90 E is right. Layers show LOLA elevation, derived coarse slope and Diviner historical summer maximum bolometric brightness temperature. Three preset study coordinates support A/B comparison, zoom, Kelvin/Celsius, and saved notes. Numeric preset elevation and temperature are sampled from native sources, rather than resampled overview pixels. There is no arbitrary-point query or imagery layer. The visible terrain layer is a color rendering of instrument elevation, not a photograph. This is a coarse regional reference, not a landing safety assessment.

LOLA LDEM_16 V3.1: 16 pixels/degree, 2009-07-13–2016-11-29, signed little-endian int16, elevation = DN × 0.5 m above 1737.4 km radius. Source: https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/lro-l-lola-3-rdr-v1/lrolol_1xxx/data/lola_gdr/cylindrical/img/ldem_16.lbl

Diviner: `polar_south_80_summer_max-float.tif`, approximately 240 m pixels, native GeoTIFF tie point/scale and missing values preserved. Period and definition: https://luna1.diviner.ucla.edu/~jpierre/diviner/level4_polar/aareadme.txt . Data: https://luna1.diviner.ucla.edu/~jpierre/diviner/level4_polar/additional_maps/ . Credit Williams et al. (2019), Seasonal polar temperatures on the Moon, https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006028 . Cold temperatures do not establish ice.

## Orbit & Signal Lab — Lunar Signal Explorer

Open `/?explore=signal`. Simple-English navigation has three visibly different learning steps. It starts paused at Shackleton with Satellite 2 selected; Its study coordinates are independent of the post-mission report. A persistent banner identifies the page as a proposed-design simulation, not live tracking. NASA sources and methods remain accessible at the bottom.

1. **The problem:** only the Moon and study point are shown in 3D. “Check direct path” explains the direct Earth-center path in a labeled diagram. A blocked message illustration stops at the diagram’s obstruction and at the actual spherical intersection in 3D.
2. **Try a satellite:** the selected satellite and calculated orbit appear, and the camera fits the Moon–satellite view. “Check satellite route” reveals the site–satellite and satellite–Earth legs in sequence. A blocked first leg prevents the illustrative message from reaching the second. “Watch paths change” exposes paused playback and individual-leg event navigation.
3. **Compare 3 days:** three large cards replace the main 3D scene. They show direct, selected-satellite and any-satellite clear time and longest gap, in hours and rounded minutes. The conclusion is calculated for the selected site and relay over the same 72 hours; all-five visibility is a union. “Try another Moon location” opens a native full-viewport dialog with five study-coordinate cards and a current-location badge. Choosing a card applies it immediately, closes the dialog and returns to Step 1 with a fresh unchecked lesson and the selected marker; satellite and scenario time are preserved. Cancel, close and Escape keep Step 3 and the location unchanged. The modal does not dismiss on a background click; its header/footer remain visible while the three-column grid (two in compact landscape) scrolls. The miniature diagram, when space permits, describes the current instant rather than the full interval.

The diagram rearranges positions and is explicitly labeled “not to scale”; message speed is illustrative. It uses the same geometry object as the 3D presentation. Plain definitions explain Moon site and relay satellite without requiring tooltips. A clear path is described as geometric opportunity, never successful radio delivery. The full-coverage result includes its 72-hour/model restriction next to the comparison.

**Engineering details** opens a non-modal right sidebar from any learning step, temporarily replacing the lesson or comparison with the live 3D editing layout. Its width is `clamp(300px, 32vw, 420px)` (300px in compact landscape); it is a grid sibling of the scene, with a fixed header and Done footer and internally scrollable controls. The existing site/relay/time values drive the scene, three path statuses, numerical values and analysis. Selecting a site or satellite reframes the Moon and the selected satellite’s full 72-hour reference track, with aspect-aware framing; time scrubbing leaves that track in view. Pointer orbit/zoom and keyboard camera presets remain available. Done, close and Escape retain edits, restore the original learning step and return focus to the Engineering details button. The scene stays interactive and keyboard focus can move outside the panel. Opening and closing pause playback; an interrupted illustration requires manual resume. Panel Play starts playback only on request.

Engineering, location and NASA Methods use independent open states with mutually exclusive surfaces. The NASA Methods native dialog has its own bounded desktop layout and internally scrolling source content. Step changes pause playback and cancel the previous illustration while preserving site, relay and time. “Start over” restores Step 1, Shackleton, Satellite 2, initial time, 600× speed and unpinned defaults. Analysis is keyed to site and relay, and superseded workers are terminated and ignored; pending selections cannot display another site’s totals, and failed analysis provides Retry.

The source is [NASA LCRNS Reference Constellation 3.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002698), a non-final design study. Five published Moon-centered ICRF Cartesian states are propagated from 1 March 2027 00:00 UTC through 4 March. These are calculated reference trajectories, not live spacecraft or an operational relay service. `docs/orbit-signal-3d-plan.md` records the approved design; this section describes the implementation.

### Scientific pipeline

`scripts/exploration/prepare-signal.py` downloads NASA sources into `/tmp/ztb-lcrns`, verifies initial states against the source document, and propagates all five relays with SciPy DOP853. Dynamics: DE421 ephemerides; fully normalized NASA GRAIL GRGM900C degree/order 25 in MOON_PA_DE421 (gravity reference radius 1738 km, GM 4902.79996708864 km³/s²); Sun, Venus, Earth, Mars/Jupiter/Saturn barycenter gravity; spherical solar radiation pressure with the paper's 1367 W/m², Cr 1.8, 1 m² area and 850 kg mass. Atmospheric drag and solid tides are off.

Implementation interpretations: planetary GM constants come from NASA `gm_de431.tpc`; Mars/Jupiter/Saturn use barycenters and system GMs available in the planetary kernel. Lunar eclipse is a binary spherical shadow, excluding penumbra and Earth shadow. These are disclosed interpretations, not an exact recreation of the original NASA design software. No long-term stability claim is made.

NASA NAIF SPICE converts UTC to ephemeris time and transforms positions/velocities into `MOON_ME_DE421`. The body-fixed velocities include frame rotation. Exported 30-second Float64 states support cubic Hermite interpolation; no constant-rate lunar rotation or extrapolation is used. Planetocentric, east-positive study coordinates use the mean-Earth frame and a 1737.4 km spherical horizon. Lunar imagery is a NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit visual map with filled polar regions; the texture does not supply terrain geometry. Three.js maps mean-Earth `(x,y,z)` to `(x,z,-y)`, preserving handedness; map center longitude 0 corresponds to the scene +X axis.

Visibility is geometric elevation above 0° for surface links and segment/sphere clearance for relay links. Earth center is a direction reference, not a ground station. Both relay legs must be clear simultaneously. Any-relay opportunity is a union, not a sum of overlapping intervals. Delay is instantaneous path length / light speed, excluding processing, RF hardware and moving-endpoint corrections. A blocked two-leg route displays “Unavailable” rather than a deliverable signal-delay estimate. Local terrain, antenna pointing, RF loss and ground-station scheduling are excluded. The old authored radio, battery, buffer and packet score are absent from this mode; `signalModel.ts` remains only as a legacy educational model and regression fixture.

### Interfaces and lifecycle

`signalScenario.ts` validates coverage, binary shape, finite values and the SHA-256 binary hash before use. `signalGeometry.ts` contains pure visibility, ray intersection and interval calculations. `signalAnalysis.worker.ts` brackets events at 1-second spacing and refines transitions to less than 0.063 seconds without blocking rendering. Sub-second grazing opportunities between samples are not guaranteed; the lesson does not claim continuous-time coverage certification. `SignalViewport.tsx` owns the renderer, textures, scene, controls and disposal; its presentation takes `direct`, `relay` or `engineering` independently of the lesson’s `LearningStep`, alongside `ExplanationState`. The same mounted renderer, orbit geometry and cached imagery survive sidebar transitions, with ResizeObserver resizing the canvas and aspect-aware camera framing. `SignalDiagram.tsx` defines those presentation types and renders an SVG/HTML diagram with text-equivalent status. `SignalLab.tsx` orchestrates scenario playback, independently pausable explanation progress, keyed worker results and the accessible learning interface.

Blocked links are drawn as explicit dashed red path guides with a blockage cross, rather than disappearing into a zero-length ray at a surface endpoint. Guides may be drawn through the Moon for explanation and are labeled as blocked guides, not propagating radio signals. Dynamic line bounds are recomputed when their endpoints change. Picking excludes hidden satellites and respects nearer lunar occlusion; camera drags do not select objects. The clickable Earth badge uses the actual Earth position for its projected bearing and explicitly identifies an endpoint behind the camera.

“Jump to next path change” includes individual site–satellite and satellite–Earth transitions, even if the complete route remains unavailable. Complete-route intervals and union comparisons retain their original semantics. Raw telemetry and the timeline live in Engineering details. Landscape layouts reserve instruction, verdict and primary-action space; the floating guide has been removed. Short-height playback opens over the scene rather than displacing the lesson. The shared Settings button sizes to its label, and the development launcher is hidden while the shared navbar is present; its keyboard shortcut remains available. Main mission navigation is unchanged.

A readiness promise registered before data loading also awaits scene preparation and shader compilation, so the existing loading screen covers heavy work. Background preparation continues during orientation suspension. Simulation playback, camera interaction/tweens and illustrative pulse progression respect the central lifecycle store. Manual pause remains independent. Scenario playback and the short message illustration have separate pause states. Both freeze during shared suspension; opening Engineering details pauses an active illustration; closing does not resume it automatically. Reduced-motion settings show the completed explanation without moving a message. The viewport skips GPU renders while its scene and camera are idle, and skips rendering during global suspension after its initial prepared frame. Missing scientific data produces Retry/Sources; WebGL failure offers a consistently scaled X–Z fallback and the same route calculations. Signal-only data loads separately from Atlas.

### Reproduce and validate the signal scene

Create a Python virtual environment and install `scripts/exploration/requirements-signal.txt`, then run:

```sh
python scripts/exploration/prepare-signal.py
node --import ./scripts/register-workspace.mjs scripts/check-signal.mjs
pnpm run build
```

Runtime assets in `apps/web/public/exploration/signal/` total approximately 3.35 MB before transport compression, including a 2K WebP Moon texture. Raw gravity files and SPICE kernels stay outside the application bundle. The manifest records exact URLs, retrieval dates, source/artifact hashes, frames, force settings and limits.

Scientific verification: source initial-state matching; analytic harmonic gradient versus independent SciPy potential finite differences (maximum 7.8e-15 km/s²); independently stepped RK45 versus DOP853 (maximum 0.784 m position difference over 72 hours, same force implementation); withheld Hermite position probes (maximum 0.000338 m). Browser-model visibility is compared with independently calculated SPICE/dense-integrator cases and event roots at all five sites; maximum tested transition disagreement is 0.031 seconds. This establishes internal numerical agreement, not real flight accuracy or NASA certification.

At Shackleton for this particular reference study, selected SV-2 has about 63.71 hours of simultaneous two-leg geometric visibility, direct Earth-center visibility is zero, and the union across all five reference relays is 72 hours. These values apply only to this interval and spherical model, not an operational coverage guarantee.

The optional browser regression runner is `scripts/check-signal-ui.mjs` (Playwright). Set `SIGNAL_LAB_URL` for a running Vite development server, `SIGNAL_PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` if Playwright is installed outside the project, and `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE_PATH` for a custom Chromium executable. `SIGNAL_UI_REPORT` optionally saves JSON evidence; `SIGNAL_UI_SCREENSHOTS` optionally selects an existing screenshot directory. It covers all three editing entry points, live renderer geometry, full-track framing, native picker semantics, four landscape tiers, focus, suspension, repeated openings, rapid selections, analysis failure/retry and fallback.

Browser verification is recorded in `docs/signal-explorer-verification.json`. Earlier rendering and numerical checks are retained as historical evidence; the guided flow has its own dated browser results. Physical-device and hardware-GPU performance, screen readers and unassisted comprehension are not certified. Shared application preloading still emits existing CORS errors for two remote NASA carousel images; the signal data and texture are local.

## Reproduce and verify

`python3 scripts/exploration/prepare.py` requires NumPy, Pillow and SciPy; downloads about 58 MB into `/tmp/exovisionaries-exploration`, then writes ~3 MB of local grids and small WebP previews. Original labels and GeoTIFF metadata are read; no browser upstream API calls are needed. NASA geometry is fetched with curl using system TLS trust. Data script is a build-time maintenance tool, not required at runtime.

The retained legacy-model checker `node scripts/check-exploration.mjs` verifies 27 site/orbit/radio configurations, byte conservation, energy limits, relay capacity, zero-energy/no-contact runs, occultation and deterministic results. Build uses the normal `pnpm run build`. Native temperature values were independently sampled from the downloaded raster at all three preset coordinates. Browser review covers layer switching, rendered preset values, changing radios and baseline comparison, completion, compact landscape controls and return to mission home. Full original mission, physical touch devices and screen readers were not re-certified by this feature work.
